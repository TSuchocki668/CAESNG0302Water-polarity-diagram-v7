Hydrogen bonding

polarity links neighboring molecules

The left panel shows water's polarity: oxygen pulls shared electrons toward itself, giving the oxygen side of the molecule a slight negative charge (δ−) and the hydrogen sides a slight positive charge (δ+). The right panel shows what polarity makes possible. Each δ+ hydrogen end attracts the δ− oxygen end of a neighboring molecule, forming a hydrogen bond. These bonds link water molecules into a flexible, shifting network, giving water the cohesion to form droplets, carry nutrients upward through plant stems, and remain liquid across the range of temperatures where life operates.
